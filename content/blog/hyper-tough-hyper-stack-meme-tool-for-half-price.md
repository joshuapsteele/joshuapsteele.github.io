Remember when I admitted that [I'm addicted to mini toolkits](/help-im-addicted-to-mini-toolkits-the-edc-tool-struggle-is-real/)? Yeah, that hasn't gotten any better.

Quick recap: I waited most of 2024 for Harbor Freight to release the "Meme Tool w/Sockets" they teased at SEMA 2023. It finally showed up as the [ICON 1/4 in. Drive Compact Socket and Bit Set, 52-Piece](https://www.harborfreight.com/14-in-drive-compact-socket-and-bit-set-52-piece-70695.html) (the "Meme Tool 2.0," if you hang out on r/HarborFreight). It retails for $79.99, and I grabbed mine for 40% off during the August 2025 Parking Lot Sale.

I've been happy with it. Then Walmart went and did this.

## Enter the Hyper Stack

I just bought (and received) the [Hyper Tough Hyper Stack 1/4-Inch Drive Socket Set with Hard Carrying Case, SAE and Metric, 52 Pieces](https://www.walmart.com/ip/Hyper-Tough-Hyper-Stack-1-4-Inch-Drive-Socket-Set-with-Hard-Carrying-Case-SAE-and-Metric-52-Pieces/20848018071) from Walmart for $39.97.

Same piece count. Same 1/4 in. drive. Same locking flex-head ratchet, spinner handle, 2 in. and 4 in. extensions, universal joint, bit holder socket, and bit extension. Same 21 shallow sockets, from 3/16 to 9/16 in. and 5 to 14 mm, with no skipped sizes. Even the list of included accessories reads almost word for word like Harbor Freight's.

I have no inside knowledge about who makes what. But when two 52-piece kits line up this closely, it's hard not to wonder if they came out of the same factory.

## What the Hyper Stack Does Differently

The thing I like most about the Walmart kit is the ratchet. It's a pass-through design, just like the one in Harbor Freight's [original Meme Tool](https://www.harborfreight.com/locking-flex-head-ratchet-and-bit-set-35-piece-58074.html). The 4 in. extension slides through the head so you can use it like a T-handle to spin fasteners down quickly, and the 1/4 in. hex gear takes driver bits directly.

That was one of my complaints when the Meme Tool 2.0 came out. I really wish they hadn't changed up the ratchet from the first Meme Tool, and the new one doesn't pass through. So the $40 Walmart kit gets back something that the $80 Harbor Freight kit lost.

A few other things stood out to me on the Walmart side:

- The sockets have color-coded size markings, red for SAE and blue for metric. Small thing, but anyone who has squinted at a 7/32 vs. 6 mm socket in a dim garage will appreciate it.
- The case has a magnetic holder in the middle and clips into Walmart's Hyper Stack modular storage system, if you're into that sort of thing (I'm trying very hard not to be).

The bit selection is slightly different. Harbor Freight lists Torx security bits up to T40, and Walmart lists tamper-proof star bits from TT10 to TT30. If you regularly need a T40, that might matter to you.

## Meme Tool 2.1 Is Coming

Apparently I'm not the only one who missed that pass-through ratchet. At SEMA this month, Harbor Freight announced an update to the 52-piece set:

{{< youtube FvxV495LFp0 >}}

According to [Gear Patrol's write-up](https://www.gearpatrol.com/home/harbor-freight-52-piece-socket-set-meme-tool-2-update/), the updated kit will make two changes:

- It swaps in the same pass-through ratchet as the original Meme Tool.
- The spinner handle gets a 1/4 in. hex bit holder instead of a 1/4 in. square drive, so you can pop a bit straight into it.

That second change would put Harbor Freight a step ahead of Walmart, since the Hyper Stack's spinner handle is a 1/4 in. square drive like the current ICON's. (You can still use bits with a square-drive spinner by adding the bit holder socket. It's just one more piece to keep track of.)

The catch is timing. Harbor Freight hasn't announced a price or exact release date, and the updated set isn't expected in stores until summer 2027.

## What These Kits Are Still Missing: Pliers

As good as these kits are, none of them include pliers. And pliers are probably the tool I reach for most after a screwdriver.

Back in the [first post](/help-im-addicted-to-mini-toolkits-the-edc-tool-struggle-is-real/), I mentioned the 3D-printed ICAN'T case that someone on r/HarborFreight designed to hold the original Meme Tool plus sockets. In September 2024, I went down a similar road and paid $20 for a [3D-printed EDC case for the original Meme Tool](https://www.etsy.com/listing/1801021117/harbor-freight-icon-mini-ratchet-set-edc) (the ICON Locking Flex Head Ratchet and Bit Set) on Etsy.

It doesn't have the same build quality as the ICON or Hyper Tough cases. It's a 3D print, after all, and the maker is upfront that it won't be as durable as an injection-molded case. But it's thoughtfully designed and well made. It holds the original Meme Tool, room for extra 1/4 in. sockets, and the two pliers I'd most want in a kit like this:

- [KNIPEX 87 00 100 Cobra XS 4 in. Water Pump Pliers](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B08DL764J3?tag=joshuapsteele-20)
- [KNIPEX 86 04 100 Pliers Wrench XS, 4 in.](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B0B22Y62TX?tag=joshuapsteele-20)

Those two little pliers fill the biggest gap in the Meme Tool style of kit, so having them ride along in the same case is a big win. And at 6.75 x 3.75 x 1.5 in., the whole thing is still slimmer than the Hyper Stack case.

## The Case for Paying More at Harbor Freight

I don't want to pretend the cheaper kit is automatically the better buy.

The biggest thing Harbor Freight has going for it is the ICON warranty. If I break the ratchet, I can walk into any Harbor Freight and walk out with a replacement. That's a big part of why I carry their stuff in the first place.

Harbor Freight also runs sales on ICON pretty regularly. At 40% off, the ICON kit comes in at about $48, which narrows the gap to less than $10.

And the Hyper Stack is brand new. As I write this, it has only two ratings on Walmart's site, averaging 3 out of 5 stars. The ICON kit has hundreds of reviews and a whole subreddit's worth of people modding it. Time will tell how the Hyper Tough version holds up.

## So, Which One?

If you already own the ICON kit, you don't need this one. (I know. I bought it anyway.)

If you don't own either, and you're not near a Harbor Freight or can't wait for a sale, $40 for this much kit is hard to argue with. It would make a great glovebox or junk-drawer set, or a gift for someone who's just starting to build up a toolbox. And right now it's the only one of the two with a pass-through ratchet.

If you can wait until next summer, the updated ICON kit might be the best of both worlds. Plus, the current Meme Tool 2.0 will probably get marked down when the new one lands. (Will I end up buying that one too? Probably. I have a problem.)
